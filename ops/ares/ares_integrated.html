04 — THE TWO INTEGRATION TRAPS

both are invisible if you just "put them in a box"
TRAP 1 — absorbing the Zoll DOWNGRADES the altitude ceiling
Rotary-wing mountain CASEVAC regularly exceeds 15,000 ft. Absorbing the Zoll front-end makes ARES less capable than the T1 it replaces. The monitor front-end must be re-qualified to 25,000 ft — or not absorbed.
TRAP 2 — 40 litres is not a device
T1 + Zoll co-located in one shell = 320 × 264 × 471 mm → 39.8 L. That is a Pelican case, not a carryable platform. Integration must happen at the subsystem level — shared display, battery, PSU, enclosure, connectors — giving 29.6 L and 19.3 kg, not at the crate level.
Strategic read: Hamilton and Zoll already own the austere qualification (MIL-STD-810G/461F, RTCA/DO-160G, IP54/IP5X, altitude, −15→+50 °C, 2 m drop). Partnering for licensed/OEM subsystems is far faster than re-qualifying from scratch — and their test data becomes ARES evidence. That is integration, not a "system of systems", provided it is one chassis, one power architecture, one HMI, one safety governor.
